My first steps into the sailing world

April 2024, or maybe a few months later. One of those evenings when I get home from work, and my wife's on a night shift, so I've got absolutely nothing to do, I am also a little bit knackered.

YouTube's algorithm serves me up “Gli orizzonti della Maruzza” (a channel I highly recommend). Giuseppe, a guy in his fifties with a thick Florentine accent just like mine (we Tuscans can pinpoint another Tuscan's hometown just from their accent), sailing and single-handedly fixing his little 6.70 m sailboat “Maruzza” around the Greek islands and is currently planning an Atlantic crossing (there's an article on sailing YouTubers coming, don't worry).

The hype hits me.

The following week I show a couple of the videos to Cele (my wife), and that's when something dangerous happens. Very dangerous:

The hype hits her too.

You might be wondering, why dangerous? Let me explain. When I get into something, sometimes I keep dreaming about it, sometimes I make a quick rush toward it, sometimes I try it once, and then the next hype comes along and takes over. My wife? No. Oh, no. She plants herself down and, as they'd say in video games, grinds. Let me put it in a metaphor. I'm a sports car, fast on the straights, but the moment it hits a dirt road I hop off and get on a dirt bike instead, the new hobby. She's a tractor. A Landini, the old cranked, hot-headed, tracked kind, the ones you start by hand and that fire off those deafening chugs. The Landini isn't setting any lap records, but rest assured it gets exactly where it set out to go. Is there a road? Fine, chug, chug, chug. A rough, broken uphill stretch? Fine! chug, chug, chug. Muddy fields? chug, chug, chug. Straight. To. The. Destination.

So basically, if it were just me obsessing, I'd probably read a book or two, watch some videos, maybe do a week holiday with a skipper. But now she's also obsessing, and you can bet we're getting our asses onto a sailboat.

So off we g… oh wait, we're pregnant!

So between 2024 and 2025 we have what will be our last adventurous holidays for a good few years, and get ready for Fosco's arrival. And once Fosco actually arrives, we spend the first few months figuring out what exactly happened to our lives.

So that's it, nothing's happening then? Shh. Do you hear that background noise? chug, chug, chug. The tractor is coming. Nothing stops the tractor.

What can you possibly do with a newborn who keeps you up all night and needs constant attention all day? Get your boating license! (as any sane person would obviously respond)

Is that the ideal place to start sailing? Probably not. Some hands-on boat experience first would be wiser. But in our case, with a newborn, it's a bit of a hard sell convincing a skipper to take on a tiny human who keeps everyone awake and produces diapers like a machine gun, whereas the theory can at least be studied from home.

Fine, so at least we're getting the basic, easy license, right? Absolutely not! Category A, sail and motor, no limit, boats up to 24 meters! Because, keep in mind, we're not doing this sailing thing for fun; we're planning ocean crossings, on our own boat! Tomorrow? No. Next year? No. But the tractor gets there eventually.

So we start calling sailing schools until we find one that doesn't hang up on us, and it just so happens that on a cold afternoon in November 2025, someone waiting in line to renew their own boating license overhears the following conversation (obviously just their end of the call):
“Oh, from Germany?”
“Oh, with a baby? Well, I guess the wife's got him…”
“Oh, both of you? Hmm… and how old is the baby?”
“Well, I guess it can be done…”
“What do you mean, ‘months, not years’?!”

Studying carthography with a toddler

And so, from the start of 2026, we begin studying with an Italian sailing school that runs online courses. They're not exactly easy to follow, because no matter how hard you try, a baby that age only falls asleep with a boob, and I, unfortunately, do not have boobs. Naturally, the classes are in the evening, the exact same time you'd want to be putting Fosco to bed.

And what about the practical training? Yes, unsurprisingly the license needs practicals too. We try to cram them into trips to Viareggio, taking turns with one of us watching the baby, the other in sailing class. Passing our son back and forth, with help from the grandparents, we manage our first 15 hours each (separately) on an 37 foot Daphne sailboat, learning the basics of jibing, tacking, mooring, and man-overboard recovery (simulated with fenders), then in the evenings we compare opinions on instructors and try to remember what that rope was called again (sorry, “line”). Studying theory and chart plotting in whatever scraps of time are left over from work and our son, constantly interrupted.

Back from a lesson

So how does it end? Well, we don't know yet! We've passed the theory exam (to be fair, Cele has to retake one multiple-choice section she didn't pass, only because with a kid, a mother doesn't exactly have unlimited study time), and we're now waiting on the practical exam in September 2026. In the meantime, we're on the hunt for a sailing club on the lakes around Munich to keep practicing maneuvers, and instead of sexting each other, we send photos of boats for sale that we could or definitely cannot afford.

And then what?

We still have plenty of open questions. Do we drop everything and live on a boat Or take holidays there? Buy or charter? Lake or sea?

Meanwhile, the bookshelf keeps filling up with books on ocean crossings, boat repair manuals, and offshore tactics.

And here I am, still dreaming of the Atlantic. But this is Cele's dream too. Chug, Chug, Chug…
